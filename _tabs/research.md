### Mental State and Pragmatic Reasoning 

##### One Example: Theory of Mind and Autism Pragmatics 

A lot of times, autism is considered a communicative disorder. A lot of times, this is described as autistic people struggling with pragmatics. A lot of times, this is said to be due to a lack of theory of mind. Yet these are strong assumptions that may or may not be true. What defines theory of mind or pragmatic performance? Which autistic groups — or subgroups — are we talking about? How much theory of mind does pragmatics actually involve? Do autistic people really struggle with pragmatics? Do they really struggle with theory of mind? In my research, I hope to revisit some of these existing assumptions about pragmatics in autism.

- My undergraduate thesis centers on if and how differences in information processing between autistic and neurotypical adults influence their choices in referential expression production. [Here](/assets/202608_perepaa_poster.pdf){:target="_blank"}'s what it currently looks like.
- I have also worked on a proposal thinking about the case of pragmatics (and communication in general) in autistic people focusing on the differences in experiences rather than theory of mind. This work was done with [Dezhi Luo](https://ihzedoul.com/){:target="_blank"}. 

### Mental State and Mental State Language 

While broadly defined as "the ability to reason other's mental states", what theory of mind actually entails and how it's involved in language remains complicated (well at least I think it's complicated). I am very interested in the breakdown of theory of mind, how it's developed, and how language/communication and mental states interact with each other.
